The answer is computed from the breeder's equation and validation bandwidth, not asserted. The sliders below are the model: drag them and the numbers and curve change at once.

What "wet lab" means in breeding: the genotyping laboratory (extraction, hybridisation, scanning), phenotype capture (weighing, imaging, feed-intake sensors) and reproduction execution (insemination scheduling, hatching, embryo transfer). On the human line: methylation arrays, HLA typing and immunogenicity assays. Automation changes human latency and compliance, not biology's clock: gestation and growth do not shorten because a robot is involved.
For breeders: your three familiar pains are all in the model: only part of the advice gets executed (compliance); not every animal returns a phenotype (coverage); results wait a generation plus manual collation (latency). Automation pushes those three towards 100 %, 100 % and the biological minimum; the generation interval moves only with genotyping at hatch or in-vitro generations.
For AI people: N(t) = N0 + S·coverage·max(0, t − latency); r = 0.9·√(N h² / (N h² + Me)); ΔG = i·[c·r + (1 − c)·r0]; bandwidth = S·coverage / latency; false promotions = P·10·α without the judge versus ≤ α with it. Note that accuracy saturates after a few thousand reference animals: automation's value is not "more accurate" but faster, more complete, more compliant — and still no false positives when proposals scale tenfold.

The four quantities automation can move

Compliance

The share of advice that gets executed.

1 % in Demo 3's simulator, tens of percent in practice; close to 100 % once advice flows straight into the reproduction system. Non-compliant matings follow the manual index (r0) in the model.

Phenotype coverage

The share of animals that return a phenotype.

Weighing, imaging and feed-intake sensors bring coverage near everyone; coverage sets how many samples enter the reference each year.

Label latency

Years from selection to a usable phenotype.

Manual collation pushes latency beyond a generation; automatic capture brings it down to the biological minimum. Latency divides straight into validation bandwidth.

Generation interval

Years from a parent's birth to its offspring's birth.

Only genotyping at hatch (earlier selection) or in-vitro generations (cattle: embryo genotyping + IVF) move it; the largest lever on the cattle line.

Why automation needs the judge

The cheaper ideas get, the more candidates enter the judge each year. With one 5 % significance test per candidate, the broiler S2 scenario would admit {{d5.fp_no_s2}} false positives into breeding plans over ten years; with the judge's correction for the number of attempts the family-wise error stays below {{d5.alpha}} — the mechanism behind Demo 1's zero promoted negative controls and every rejection in Demo 0. Automation multiplies validation bandwidth; the judge decides whether that bandwidth becomes conclusions or noise.

Boundaries

Biology's clock stays

Gestation, incubation and growth are hard floors; the model's minimum latency is exactly that. Any claim that "automation makes generations faster" is false unless it rests on earlier selection or in-vitro generations.

Welfare, regulation, biosecurity

Absent from the model and possibly the real bottleneck. The compliance path for automated mating and early genotyping needs its own case.

No cost term

Without equipment and labour costs this page is not a return-on-investment table; that waits for automation quotes and customer capacity data.
