Glossary术语表：二十几个词，读懂本站每一页。

After this page a reader halfway into the field can place every term in the four demos and on the dashboard. Each entry says what the thing is and why it matters, without derivations.

Breeding and genetics

TermWhat it isWhy it matters
Breeding value (EBV / GEBV)The estimated genetic merit an animal passes to its offspring; the G prefix means it was estimated from genomic markers.Selection is ranking by it; "is the ranking accurate" throughout this site refers to it.
ssGBLUPSingle-step genomic best linear unbiased prediction: pedigree and genomic relationships combined into one matrix to estimate breeding values — the industry standard.The site's frozen champion. Any new method must prove itself more accurate than this to count.
Accuracy rThe correlation between predicted breeding values and true ones (known in simulation) or later-realised ones, from 0 to 1.The r term of the breeder's equation; raising it is genomic selection's main contribution.
Breeder's equation ΔG = i·r·σA / LGenetic gain per year = selection intensity × accuracy × genetic standard deviation ÷ generation interval.Demo 1b splits "+50 % per year" into these four terms to see how much each can move.
Generation interval LAverage years from a parent's birth to its offspring's birth (about 1 for chickens, 1.5 for pigs, 3 for cattle).Shortening it is genomic selection's second contribution, and the source of "time is the moat": every ledger record waits a generation to return.
Daetwyler boundThe theoretical accuracy ceiling √(N·h² / (N·h² + Me)) from reference size N, heritability h² and the number of effective chromosome segments Me.A measurement above it can only be leakage; Demo 1 treats it as a hard rule.
Reference populationThe animals with both genotypes and phenotypes that train the breeding-value model.Bigger is more accurate (curve in Demo 1); pooling references across customers is what "cross-customer learning" means.
LR method (ρ, μ, b)Legarra & Reverter 2018: compare predictions from partial data with predictions from full data to get correlation ρ, bias μ and dispersion b.b should be near 1; departures mean breeding values are systematically inflated or compressed. "Dispersion" on the dashboard is this.
Inbreeding / ΔFThe rate at which homozygosity rises through mating of relatives.The plan-level gate caps it: no ranking, however accurate, may buy gain with inbreeding.
ImputationInferring untyped loci from the loci a low-density array did measure."Low-density solid-phase panel + imputation" is the economics of the array route.
MAFMinor allele frequency.Low-MAF loci are hard to impute and hard to estimate; Demo 1 reports imputation accuracy by MAF.
Hypothesis factoryThe loop in which four agent roles (Geneticist, Builder, Critic, Analyst) propose, implement, criticise and debrief; they can only recommend and change no state.Demo 0 replays it; once hypotheses are cheap, validation bandwidth is what is scarce.
Leak probeA hypothesis that "peeks at the future", planted deliberately among the proposals to test whether the Critic stops it.The hard agent-reliability metric: all must be stopped; Demo 0's Critic quiz uses them.
ComplianceThe share of issued mating advice that is actually executed.One of the four quantities automation can move: non-compliant matings follow the manual index and dilute genetic gain directly (Demo 5).
Label latencyYears from selection to a usable phenotype; the biological minimum is a hard floor.Divides straight into validation bandwidth; manual collation pushes it past a generation, automatic capture brings it to the floor (Demo 5).
Generation interval LMean years from a parent's birth to its offspring's birth.The denominator of the breeder's equation; only earlier selection or in-vitro generations move it, robots do not (Demo 5's boundary).

Judge and validation

TermWhat it isWhy it matters
Champion / challengerThe champion is the frozen incumbent method; a challenger is anything that wants to replace it.Promotion requires a paired win on the same candidates and the same cutoff date.
Forward validationOnly past data predict later generations (or later batches, or other patients).Random cross-validation is inflated by relatedness; forward validation is what actually happens in industry.
Sealed generation (holdout)The last generation's data are locked, unreadable by agents, opened once when a campaign ends.Prevents peeking of any kind.
Paired increment ΔOOSThe accuracy difference between challenger and champion on the same held-out animals, with a bootstrap interval.The incremental gate: the lower bound must exceed 0. "More accurate" can only be shown this way.
Negative controlThe same procedure run after shuffling the labels (arm E) or dropping loci at random (arm F).Promotions must be zero, or the judge itself does not hold — the falsifier of the whole method.
Leak probeA deliberately planted hypothesis that uses future information.The Critic must intercept it before any code is written; the interception rate is the core "agent reliability" number.
Multiple testing / DSR-style deflationWith many candidates the best one passes by luck; the bar rises with the number of attempts.The research gate and Demo 4's Bonferroni correction both mean this.
Six gatesValidity, accuracy, paired increment, plan level (ΔF and gain), robustness (by farm / batch stratum), research (multiple testing).Only gates change a candidate's state; agents only recommend.
Arms A–GThe seven legs of Demo 1's one controlled experiment: baseline, random search, one-shot AI, the full loop, two negative controls, an external prior.They are not the four demos. The letter in the middle of a campaign id on the dashboard is the arm.
Validation bandwidthSamples entering the ledger per year ÷ years of label latency.Industry counts in millions, laboratories in thousands; the quantified form of "time is the moat".

Read-out and memory

TermWhat it isWhy it matters
LedgerAn append-only record: every recommendation, adoption and outcome one generation later, traceable per animal.The carrier of the data asset and the customer's switching cost; three tables in Demo 3.
Randomised control matingRandom assignment decides which pairs follow the advice and which serve as controls.The only way observational data become an experiment; the ledger already has the field.
Methylation clockAge estimated from methylation levels at a set of CpG sites; the cross-species version uses relative age = age / maximum lifespan.Line D's read-out; it measures age, not lifespan.
Age accelerationClock prediction minus the expectation at chronological age.Only when it links to an outcome (productive life, treatment effect) does "biological age" carry commercial meaning.
Conserved CpGA site whose sequence is identical across several species.A cross-species array can carry only these; the screening threshold sets the panel size.
HLA typing / imputationInferring four-digit human leukocyte antigen alleles from an SNP panel.The wedge into the human line; rare-allele accuracy depends on the population reference panel.
Neoantigen rankingOrdering the candidate peptides produced by tumour mutations by how likely they are to trigger immunity.The subject of line E audits; the selling point is reproducible, leak-free, comparable — not "more accurate".
Pre-freeze independent validationAn algorithm cannot change for 5–7 years once in the clinic; before the freeze a third party validates it on held-out data.Demo 4's report format: protocol, hashes, gates and results only.
