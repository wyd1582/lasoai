For breeders: think of the AI as four assistants with separate jobs: one proposes ideas (the Geneticist), one turns an idea into a runnable computation (the Builder), one looks for holes and stops ideas that peek at future data (the Critic), one writes the debrief (the Analyst). None of them can change the breeding plan; every change of state passes six gates.
For AI people: the four roles map to prompts P1–P5 with JSON-schema outputs; they may call only deterministic tools (GRM, BLUP, LD, annotation) and produce candidates and hypotheses, never numbers. The Critic's leak scan stops hypotheses that use information after selection_date before any code is written; deduplication is by semantic hash of the DSL; budgets cap both tokens and full evaluations. The replay below is the real events.jsonl and registry.
For the board: generating hypotheses is already cheap; validation is scarce. Four roles, one iron rule

Geneticist · proposes

Reads the data catalogue and the ledger summary, proposes a hypothesis with a clear mechanism, direction and falsifiers, and writes it as an operator plan (DSL).

May see: data fields, available priors, what the ledger has tried. May not see: the sealed generation, any future phenotype.

Builder · implements

Turns the plan into executable DSL with a data declaration (when each field becomes available) and four self-checks (grammar and units, causality, replay determinism, edge cases).

Returns "operator needed" when the language lacks one; never invents data.

Critic · finds holes

Reviews once at the hypothesis stage and once at the code stage: temporal, relatedness and structural leaks, thesis–code mismatch, dispersion risk.

It is the first gate and the source of the "agent reliability" number: every deliberately planted leak probe must be stopped.

Analyst · debriefs

After evaluation, writes in plain language which gate stopped the candidate, by how much, and which experiment is worth running next.

Its notes go into the daily digest and the BreedingPackage; it changes no state.

The iron rule: agents only recommend; a candidate's state changes only through the six deterministic gates (validity, accuracy, paired increment, plan level, robustness, research). The orange "gate" steps in the replay are those state changes — decided by numbers, not by the AI.

Why "0 promoted" is good news

The judge is working

Of the {{d0.evaluated}} candidates that went through every gate in this replay, none produced a reproducible increment over the frozen champion; across all of Demo 1 (simulation + pig data) challengers promoted {{d1.promoted_total}} and negative controls slipped through {{d1.neg_sim}} / {{d1.neg_pig}} times. Review-table row 6 said it already: linear models are hard to beat at scale.

Every rejection is an asset

The rejected idea, the gate and the margin all go into the ledger. When the Geneticist reads the ledger summary next round it avoids tried clusters and turns to untried ones — cheap hypotheses become a non-repeating search.

Bandwidth is the bottleneck

Ideas come ten a minute; one full evaluation runs a complete forward validation, and the truth arrives a generation later. So the effort goes into validation bandwidth and the judge, not into "a model that proposes better". That is the starting point of the self-driving what-if.
