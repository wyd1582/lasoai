For breeders: this is what you do every cycle, now written down: arrays arrive → genotype QC → reference-population update → breeding values → selection and mating advice → which advice you took → progeny performance one generation later. Any animal's breeding value traces back to the batch it came from, the retraining that produced it and the rule that ranked it.
For AI people: the ledger is three tables — recommendations / decisions / outcomes — plus data snapshots and views; every recommendation carries issued_at, every decision decided_at, every outcome available_at, and forward filling is forbidden. The randomized_control field is reserved for randomised control matings: the path from observational data to an experiment. The same GBLUP formula recomputed independently agrees with the stored breeding values per animal — the mechanism behind "per-animal agreement in a shadow run".
For the board: the deck's customer-case page reads "before: manual analysis, weeks, unpaid, results in e-mails and spreadsheets; after: on-premise deployment, ≤ 4 weeks, paid contract, traceable per animal, improving cycle over cycle". This page is the technical shape of "after". The ledger is the switching cost and the starting point of the three compounding loops.

That is simulator behaviour, not an estimate of real adoption. Next: let the mating node actually drive the simulator's parent choice; after that, connect the customer's workflow.

Validation bandwidth

bandwidth = samples_per_year / label_latency_years = {{d3.samples_per_year}} / {{d3.latency}} = {{d3.bandwidth}}, assuming one batch = one generation ≈ one year and a one-generation label delay. Scaled by the simulated arrival interval (about 98 days per batch), samples per year come to about {{d3.bandwidth_calendar}}. The deck's framing: industry counts millions of samples a year, laboratories thousands of experiments; our moat is time, theirs is compute.

Commercial meaning

Line A · breeding agent

An on-premise selection loop, deployment fee plus annual fee. This page is the skeleton of the deliverable: seven steps, traceable per animal, reproducible at every step.

Line B · data platform and ledger

Platform fee plus per-sample. Every generation adds a labelled layer to the ledger; a latecomer starts three generations behind. Retention is assumed ≥ 90 % because the ledger is the switching cost.

Three compounding loops

Data (labels accumulate with generations), content (the ledger reveals which loci keep contributing prediction → the next panel), trust (reproducible, auditable records adopted by customers and regulators). All three start from these three tables.

Where the two cash-layer numbers come from: the mechanism behind "per-animal agreement ≥ 99 %" is this page's independent recomputation ({{d3.agree}} / {{d3.agree_n}}, largest difference {{d3.max_diff}} g); the mechanism behind "delivery ≤ 4 weeks" is a pipeline that rebuilds with one command (this demo: {{d3.seconds}} s). Turning both into real promises needs only a shadow run on customer data.
